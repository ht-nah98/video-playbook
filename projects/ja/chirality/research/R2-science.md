## A. Pasteur, 1848
- **Date:** The memoir was presented to the Académie des Sciences on **22 May 1848**. [V] Hervé This, *C. R. Chimie* 25 (2022) 237, English translation: https://doi.org/10.5802/crchim.179-en (PDF: https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/chimie/article/en/10.5802/crchim.179.pdf)
- **Age:** Pasteur was born **27 Dec 1822** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur), so he was **25** in May 1848. At the time he was a young *agrégé préparateur* at the École Normale Supérieure. [V] Vantomme & Crassous, "Pasteur and chirality: a story of how serendipity favors the prepared minds", *Chirality* 33 (2021) 597: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9291139
- **The salt:** the **sodium ammonium salt** of racemic acid ("paratartrate"). Berzelius named it "paratartaric acid" and Gay-Lussac named it "racemic acid". [V] Vantomme & Crassous (above).
- **Origin of racemic acid:** discovered by the Alsatian industrialist **Charles Kestner** in Thann, Alsace, in **1822** ("thannic acid"/"paratartaric acid"), as a by-product of his tartaric-acid works. [V] This 2022 (above). Wikipedia says racemic acid "does not occur naturally in grape juice, although L-tartaric acid does" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_acid). So "from wine lees" should be told as: tartar from wine-making → Kestner's factory → racemic acid. See MYTHS.
- **Temperature, the "luck":** the sodium ammonium salt crystallizes as a **conglomerate** (separate + and − crystals) **below 27.7 °C**, and as a racemic compound above that. "Pasteur was fortunate in his selection of this salt for observation, and in the conditions under which he prepared it." The method has seldom worked for other racemates because "the necessary crystal modification is relatively rare." [V] https://organicchemistrydata.org/reusch/virtualtext/resolution-of-racemates/
  - Vantomme & Crassous put it differently: conglomerate at 20 °C, racemate at 30 °C, and the conglomerate dehydrates at 28 °C. "By chance, he could have missed it!" [V] PMC9291139
  - Textbooks round this to "below 28 °C". [S] https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/5-4-pasteurs-discovery-of-enantiomers
- **Hemihedral facets:** "each crystal has a tiny facet on one of its edges oriented sometimes to the right and sometimes to the left". He separated them by hand with tweezers. Vantomme & Crassous call it "the first artificial chiral resolution in the history of science". [V] PMC9291139
- **Polarimeter:** each sorted pile was optically active, with rotations **equal in magnitude and opposite in sign**. The original 50:50 mixture was inactive. [S] OpenStax (above) and https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Organic_Chemistry/Organic_Chemistry_(OpenStax)/05%3A_Stereochemistry_at_Tetrahedral_Centers/5.04%3A_Pasteur's_Discovery_of_Enantiomers
- **Biot:** the puzzle came from **Mitscherlich**, reported through Biot. Mitscherlich said the sodium ammonium tartrate and paratartrate were identical except that one rotated light and the other did not. [S] PMC9291139 search excerpt. Pasteur then **repeated the experiment together with Biot**: "This important discovery was later reproduced together with Biot". [V] PMC9291139. Biot's remark, in the original French: "Mon cher enfant, j'ai tant aimé les sciences dans ma vie que cela me fait battre le cœur!" (https://blogs.ch.cam.ac.uk/pmr/2009/08/19/mon-cher-enfant-jai-tant-aime-les-sciences-dans-ma-vie-que-cela-me-fait-battre-le-coeur/). In that account Biot saw the strong laevorotation in the Soleil saccharimeter. [S] The career effect is in Mike Sutton, Chemistry World: https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/ethoxylation-gets-long-chain-surfactants-to-dissolve-at-ambient-temperatures/4016497.article (the URL slug is odd, but the search engine gave it as Sutton's Pasteur bicentenary piece). [S]
- **Other credit:** Auguste **Laurent** taught Pasteur crystallography plus polarimetry and supplied the crystals. Pasteur rarely credited him. [V] This 2022.
- Japanese re-enactments found the crystallization "not very reproducible". [S] Racemic acid, Wikipedia.

## B. Kelvin coins "chiral"
- **Where and when:** the Second **Robert Boyle Lecture**, given to the **Oxford University Junior Scientific Club** on **Tuesday 16 May 1893**. It was printed as *The Molecular Tactics of a Crystal* (Clarendon Press, Oxford, **1894**). [V] Project Gutenberg title page: https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/54976 (text: https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/54976/pg54976.txt)
- **Quote (footnote 8 in the 1894 text, also footnote to §22):** "I call any geometrical figure, or group of points, *chiral*, and say that it has chirality, if its image in a plane mirror, ideally realized, cannot be brought to coincide with itself. Two equal and similar right hands are homochirally similar. Equal and similar right and left hands are heterochirally similar or 'allochirally' similar (but heterochirally is better)." [V] Gutenberg text.
- **1904:** the same footnote is reprinted word for word in Appendix H of the ***Baltimore Lectures*** (C. J. Clay/Cambridge University Press, 1904). Lecture XX there also uses "chiral" for optical rotation. [V] https://archive.org/details/baltimorelecture00kelviala. **Coinage = 1893 (published 1894). 1904 is only a reprint.**
- **Etymology:** Ancient Greek **χείρ (kheír), "hand"**. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality
- Note: the Rzepa/Imperial page dates it **1873**, which is wrong. [S]

## C. Tetrahedral carbon, 1874
- **van 't Hoff** published a Dutch pamphlet in **fall (September) 1874**: *Voorstel tot Uitbreiding der Tegenwoordige in de Scheikunde gebruikte Structuurformules in de Ruimte*. An expanded French version, *La chimie dans l'espace*, followed in **1875**. Carbon's four bonds point to the corners of a regular tetrahedron, and this explains optical activity. **Le Bel** reached the same idea independently in 1874 (van 't Hoff from bond directions, Le Bel from the equilibrium of the four radicals). van 't Hoff won the **first** Nobel Prize in Chemistry (**1901**), though for a different subject. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Henricus_van_%27t_Hoff · https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/jacobus-henricus-van-t-hoff/
- **Why four different groups give a mirror twin:** a carbon with four **different** groups (an "asymmetric carbon") has no mirror plane, so its mirror image cannot be superimposed on it. It exists as two distinct forms. If any two groups are the same, a mirror plane exists and the molecule is identical to its mirror image. [S] Wikipedia van 't Hoff, plus standard textbook geometry. Fine to show as an animation.

## D. Homochirality, and L/D is not the direction of rotation
- Life uses **L-amino acids** (19 of the 20 proteinogenic amino acids). **Glycine is achiral.** Life uses **D-sugars**: ribose in RNA, deoxyribose in DNA. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homochirality · Soai's own summary: "life on the Earth is composed of L-amino acids and D-sugars" [V] https://www.rs.kagu.tus.ac.jp/soai/Soai_Research_Work.pdf
- **L/D ≠ left/right rotation:** D/L gives configuration **relative to glyceraldehyde** (Fischer). The (+)/(−) sign is a measured property and "does not correlate with its configuration". [S] https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Organic_Chemistry/Basic_Principles_of_Organic_Chemistry_(Roberts_and_Caserio)/19%3A_More_on_Stereochemistry/19.05%3A_Absolute_And_Relative_Configuration
  - "not all L-amino acids are levorotatory and the actual direction of light rotation can vary with amino acid" [V] https://biosci.mcdb.ucsb.edu/biochemistry/tw-amn/aas-stereo.htm
  - Sugars: D-glucose is dextrorotatory, while **D-fructose is levorotatory**. [S] LibreTexts D/L sugars page (search result).
  - **Warning:** the English Wikipedia *Homochirality* article itself links L/D to rotation direction ("L- levorotatory, D- dextrorotatory"). Do NOT copy that.
  - Lowercase d/l (= +/−) is a different, IUPAC-discouraged notation. Keep the script to capital **D/L** or to **R/S**.
- Nobel/AP framing: "DNA twists to the right, and human proteins are built mostly from left-handed building blocks." [S] ADN/AP.

## E. Carvone and limonene
- **(R)-(−)-carvone = spearmint** (50–80% of spearmint oil). **(S)-(+)-carvone = caraway** (60–70% of caraway oil) and about **40–60% of dill seed oil**. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carvone · original assignment: Friedman & Miller / Leitereg et al. 1971, PubMed https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/5573954/ [S]
- About 8% of people cannot smell (R)-(−)-carvone, which points to enantioselective odorant receptors (OR1A1 study). [S] https://portal.fis.leibniz-lsb.tum.de/en/publications/structural-determinants-of-a-conserved-enantiomer-selective-carvo-4/
- **Limonene "orange vs lemon" is a MYTH.** Kvittingen, Sjursnes & Schmid, "Limonene in Citrus: A String of Unchecked Literature Citings?", *J. Chem. Educ.* 2021, doi:10.1021/acs.jchemed.1c00363. In two odor tests, **few** people linked analytical-grade (R)-(+)-limonene with orange or (S)-(−)-limonene with lemon. The claim traces back to "an often quoted article from 1971", and the authors say textbooks "should be corrected". Both orange and lemon oils are dextrorotatory: lemon oil is not (S)-limonene, and orange oil is more than 98% (R)-(+)-limonene. [V] https://diverdi.colostate.edu/C442/references/other/acs.jchemed.1c00363.pdf
- The ACS Reactions infographic: both fruits contain mostly (R)-limonene, with only 1–4% (S). Pure (S) smells turpentine-like with some lemon. [S] https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/pressroom/reactions/infographics/why-do-oranges-and-lemons-smell-different.pdf
- → **Use carvone, not limonene, on screen.**

## F. Thalidomide
- **Launch:** **October 1957**, West Germany, brand **Contergan** (Chemie Grünenthal), sold as a sedative/hypnotic. **Japan:** **January 1958**, brand **イソミン** (Isomin), later also in the stomach remedy **プロバンM**. [V] MHLW hearing document, submitted by 財団法人いしずえ (2010-07-16): https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/shingi/2r9852000000rwbu-att/2r9852000000rwjg.pdf
- **Warning and withdrawal:** **Lenz** warned in **Nov 1961**, and the West German maker decided to withdraw it that month. **Japan stopped sales and began recall in Sept 1962**, **10 months after West Germany**. Shipments had been paused on **17 May 1962**, but the recall only started on 13 Sept 1962 (per a search summary of the same corpus). Because the recall was incomplete, affected children were still born after the stop. [V] MHLW doc (17 May/13 Sept from a search snippet [S]).
- **Victims:** worldwide "more than 10,000, and potentially up to 20,000", of whom "approximately 40 percent died at or shortly after the time of birth". [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalidomide_scandal. The MHLW/いしずえ figures: worldwide "数千人～1万人". Japan: about **1,000** fetuses including stillbirths, of whom **309 surviving victims were officially recognized** (男171・女138 per a search snippet [S]). [V] MHLW doc. The Japanese defendants in the 1974 settlement were the state (厚生省) and **大日本製薬**. [V] MHLW doc.
- **Enantiomers:** the (S)-enantiomer is linked to teratogenicity and the (R)-enantiomer to sedation. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalidomide
- **In-vivo interconversion (the key correction):** Eriksson, Björkman, Roth, Fyge & Höglund, *Chirality* 7 (1995) 44–52, doi:10.1002/chir.530070109, PMID 7702998. [V] abstract via Europe PMC:
  - In human blood at 37 °C, chiral inversion rate constants were **0.30 h⁻¹ (R)** and **0.31 h⁻¹ (S)**, a half-life of about **2.3 h** (my calculation: ln2/0.30).
  - In vivo in humans: **0.17 h⁻¹ R→S** (t½ ≈ **4 h**) and **0.12 h⁻¹ S→R** (t½ ≈ 6 h). Elimination: 0.079 h⁻¹ (R) and 0.24 h⁻¹ (S).
  - Authors: "Putative differences in therapeutic or adverse effects between (+)-(R)- and (−)-(S)-thalidomide would to a large extent be **abolished by rapid interconversion in vivo**." → **A pure (R) drug would NOT have prevented the disaster.**
- **Cereblon:** Ito T, Ando H, Suzuki T, Ogura T, Hotta K, Imamura Y, Yamaguchi Y, **Handa H**, "Identification of a primary target of thalidomide teratogenicity", ***Science* 327 (2010) 1345–1350**, doi:10.1126/science.1177319. First affiliation: **Integrated Research Institute, Tokyo Institute of Technology (東京工業大学), Yokohama**. CRBN forms an E3 ubiquitin ligase with DDB1 and Cul4A, and thalidomide binding inhibits it. The abstract notes thalidomide "is still used in the treatment of leprosy and multiple myeloma". [V] Europe PMC PMID 20223979
- **Enantiomer and cereblon:** Mori T, Ito T, … Shibata N, Handa H, Hakoshima T, *Sci. Rep.* 2018, doi:10.1038/s41598-018-19202-7. They used **deuterium-substituted** thalidomides to suppress interconversion. **(S) binds CRBN about 10-fold more strongly** than (R), and (S) caused stronger fin malformations in zebrafish. The authors are all at Japanese institutions (NAIST, Tokyo Medical University, Tokyo Tech, and others). [V] Europe PMC.
- **Current use:** US FDA approved it for erythema nodosum leprosum (**leprosy**) in **July 1998**, and for multiple myeloma (with dexamethasone) in **2006**. **Japan re-approved it for multiple myeloma on 16 Oct 2008.** [V] MHLW doc for 1998 and 2008. 2006: [S] Wikipedia *Thalidomide* (whose lead contradicts its body text; the body says 1998 = ENL, 2006 = myeloma).

## G. Kagan 1986: non-linear effects
- **Paper:** C. Puchot, O. Samuel, E. Duñach, S. Zhao, C. Agami, H. B. Kagan, "Nonlinear effects in asymmetric synthesis. Examples in asymmetric oxidations and aldolization reactions", ***J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 108 (1986) 2353–2357**, doi:10.1021/ja00269a036. [V] Crossref.
- **The three reactions** [V] MacMillan group-meeting slides (J. Wiener 2003), https://macmillan.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/Wiener_Nonlinear-effects.pdf:
  1. **Sharpless epoxidation of geraniol** (Ti(OiPr)₄, (R,R)-DET, tBuOOH, −20 °C): **(+)-NLE**, i.e. product ee is higher than linear.
  2. **Kagan's sulfide oxidation** (modified Sharpless reagent, Ti/DET/H₂O): **(−)-NLE** up to about 70% ee of ligand, then linear. Later modelled as (ML)₄ (Guillaneux et al. *JACS* 1994, 116, 9430).
  3. **Hajos–Parrish (proline) intramolecular aldol**: (−)-NLE reported (Agami). **Later contested:** Hoang, Bahmanyar, Houk & List (*JACS* 2003, 125, 16), "Kinetic and stereochemical evidence for the involvement of only one proline molecule…", and Barbas reported **no NLE**. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proline-catalyzed_aldol_reactions · Denmark group slides https://denmarkgroup.web.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/gm-2006-0725.pdf
- **ML₂ model** [V] Wiener slides; from Guillaneux, Zhao, Samuel, Rainford & Kagan, *JACS* 1994, 116, 9430:
  ee_prod = EE₀ · ee_aux · (1 + β)/(1 + gβ)
  - β = relative amount of heterochiral (meso) ML_R·ML_S to homochiral complexes = z/(x+y)
  - g = k_RS/k_RR, the relative reactivity of meso vs homochiral
  - Linear when β = 0 or g = 1. **g < 1** (meso less reactive) → **(+)-NLE**. **g > 1** → **(−)-NLE**.
  - (The slide labels these two cases backwards in one place. The maths above is correct.)
- **Reservoir effect** (Kagan): racemic or nonreactive aggregates lock up part of the ligand, so the active catalyst's effective ee rises. ee_eff = (ee_cat − α·ee_res)/(1 − α). Worked example: 60% ee ligand with 30% stored as racemic aggregate gives **86% ee** effective. [V] Wiener slides. Hawbaker & Blackmond call the off-cycle inactive-dimer picture the "**Noyori reservoir**" model, as opposed to Kagan's ML₂ where the dimers themselves are the catalysts. [V] arXiv:1909.13015
- **Noyori DAIB (1989):** Kitamura, Okada, Suga & Noyori, "Enantioselective addition of dialkylzincs to aldehydes promoted by chiral amino alcohols. Mechanism and nonlinear effect", ***JACS* 111 (1989) 4028–4036**, doi:10.1021/ja00193a040 [V Crossref]. **8 mol% (−)-DAIB of 15% ee** with Et₂Zn and benzaldehyde gave **(S)-1-phenyl-1-propanol in 95% ee**. Cause: the **heterochiral (R,S) dinuclear Zn complex is much more stable and catalytically inactive**, which ties up the minor enantiomer; the homochiral dimer dissociates to the active monomer. [S] search summary of the abstract; Nagoya Univ. lab page https://www.ps.nagoya-u.ac.jp/os/en/?p=103
  - The figure "enantiopure DAIB gives 98% ee" is [M]. Check it before using.

## I. Soai
- **1990:** K. Soai, S. Niwa, H. Hori, *J. Chem. Soc., Chem. Commun.* **1990**, 982, doi:10.1039/C39900000982 [V Crossref]. **3-Pyridyl alkanol** + i-Pr₂Zn + pyridine-3-carbaldehyde: (S)-catalyst of **86% ee** gave (S)-product of only **35% ee** (67% yield). This was autocatalysis **without amplification**. [V] Kawasaki & Soai, *Bull. Chem. Soc. Jpn.* 84 (2011) 879: https://www.rs.kagu.tus.ac.jp/soai/Bull.Chem.Soc.Jpn.2011.84.879.pdf
- **1995:** K. Soai, T. Shibata, H. Morioka, K. Choji, "Asymmetric autocatalysis and amplification of enantiomeric excess of a chiral molecule", ***Nature* 378 (1995) 767–768**, doi:10.1038/378767a0 [V Crossref]. 5-Pyrimidyl alkanol, from pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde + i-Pr₂Zn, 20 mol% autocatalyst.
  - **Round-by-round ee (Table 1 of BCSJ 2011, reproducing the 1995 data): catalyst 2% → 10% (yield 46%) → 57% (75%) → 81% (80%) → 88% (75%).** So **2% → 88% ee in four rounds**. [V] BCSJ 2011 text and table; Soai, *Proc. Jpn. Acad. B* 95 (2019) 89, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541725 ("reached 88% after four consecutive asymmetric autocatalyses").
  - A 5th table entry, with 99% ee catalyst giving 88% product, appears to be a control. The table extraction was garbled, so treat that entry's meaning as uncertain.
- **Near-perfect autocatalyst:** the **2-(tert-butylethynyl)-5-pyrimidyl alkanol** gives **>99.5% ee and >99% yield**, still unchanged after 10 rounds (Shibata, Yonekubo, Soai, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 38 (1999) 659). Over the 10 rounds the original enantiomer multiplied about **60 million times**. [V] Soai_Research_Work.pdf and BCSJ 2011.
- **Extreme amplification (2003):** I. Sato, H. Urabe, S. Ishiguro, T. Shibata, K. Soai, "Amplification of Chirality from Extremely Low to Greater than 99.5% ee by Asymmetric Autocatalysis", ***Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 42 (2003) 315–317**, doi:10.1002/anie.200390105 [V Crossref].
  - **ca. 0.00005% ee → 57% → 99% → >99.5% ee in 3 rounds.**
  - 0.00005% ee is about "the difference of only several number of molecules between ca. 5 million molecules of R … and 5 million … of S". [V] Soai_Research_Work.pdf
  - The major (S) enantiomer multiplied about **630,000×**, the minor (R) less than **1,000×**. [V] BCSJ 2011 and Soai_Research_Work.pdf
- **Triggers** (all [V] from the reference list in Soai_Research_Work.pdf):
  - L-leucine of about 2% ee, the level CPL photolysis produces: *JACS* 1998, 120, 12157.
  - **d-/l-quartz** powder (d → S alkanol, l → R): *JACS* 1999, 121, 11235.
  - NaClO₃ crystals: *Angew.* 2000, 39, 1510.
  - **Deuterium** (D/H) isotopomers: *JACS* 2000, 122, 11739.
  - **Direct CPL** at 313 nm on the racemic alkanol (l-CPL → S, r-CPL → R, both >99.5% ee): *JACS* 2005, 127, 3274.
  - Cryptochiral hydrocarbon (butylethylhexylpropylmethane): *JACS* 2006, 128, 6032.
  - **¹³C/¹²C isotope chirality:** Kawasaki, Matsumura, Tsutsumi, Suzuki, Ito, Soai, ***Science* 324 (2009) 492–495**, doi:10.1126/science.1170322 [V Crossref].
  - Chiral cytosine crystals: *JACS* 2010, 132, 2874.
  - ¹⁸O/¹⁶O: *Angew.* 2011, 50, 8131.
- **Spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis (no chiral additive):** first filed as JP patent **1996** (JP 9-268179), then published as Soai et al., ***Tetrahedron: Asymmetry* 14 (2003) 185–188**. [V] references in Soai_Research_Work.pdf. Result: **(S) formed 19 times, (R) 18 times** in 37 runs, a stochastic split. Soai calls it "the first example of spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis as defined by Mislow". [V] Soai 2019 *Proc. Jpn. Acad.* full text (Europe PMC). Later stochastic runs with achiral silica gel: *Chirality* 2006, 18, 479. Another group reproduced the stochastic result (Singleton & Vo, 2002–03 [M], name not verified).
- **Coin analogy** (good for the video): "when a coin is flipped 100 times, the probability of exactly 50 heads and 50 tails is only 8%". [V] Soai 2019 and Soai_Research_Work.pdf
- **Mechanism (Blackmond):** Blackmond, McMillan, Ramdeehul, Schorm & Brown, *JACS* 123 (2001) 10103, used kinetics and reaction calorimetry. Their model:
  - **homochiral dimers of the Zn-alkoxide are the active catalysts**;
  - **heterochiral dimers are inactive** (k_hetero = 0), and that is the "mutual antagonism";
  - dimers form stochastically, RR:SS:SR = 1:1:2, which NMR confirmed (two peaks, 1:1.08).
  - The model extends Kagan ML₂ and the Noyori reservoir models. [V] Hawbaker & Blackmond, arXiv:1909.13015, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.13015
- Blackmond, "Asymmetric autocatalysis and its implications for the origin of homochirality", ***PNAS* 101 (2004) 5732**, doi:10.1073/pnas.0308363101: "The Soai reaction provided experimental confirmation of this concept, nearly 50 years after it was first proposed." [V] Europe PMC. Soai's 2019 review adds that at low ee, the amplification exceeds the dimer model, so tetramers are proposed (X-ray: Zn₂O₂ dimers bridged into a macrocycle). [V]

## J. Frank 1953
- F. C. Frank, "On spontaneous asymmetric synthesis", ***Biochim. Biophys. Acta* 11 (1953) 459–463**, doi:10.1016/0006-3002(53)90082-1. [V] Crossref.
- **Model:** (1) each enantiomer **catalyzes its own production** from an achiral substrate, and (2) each **suppresses the other** ("**mutual antagonism**"). This makes the racemic state unstable, so a tiny excess grows toward homochirality. Frank called it a "simple and sufficient life model". He ended by saying an experimental demonstration "may not be impossible". [V] Hawbaker & Blackmond (above). The standard textbook form of the equations (Frank's own notation not checked [M]):
  - dL/dt = k₁·A·L − k₂·L·D
  - dD/dt = k₁·A·D − k₂·L·D
- **Why Soai = realization:** in the Soai reaction (i) the product is its own catalyst (autocatalysis) and (ii) the minor enantiomer is sequestered in an inactive heterochiral dimer (mutual inhibition). Blackmond: Soai provided "experimental confirmation… nearly 50 years after it was first proposed". [V] PNAS 2004 abstract; Soai BCSJ 2011 also cites Frank as the model "without mentioning any chemical structures". [V]

## K. Pharma relevance
- **FDA:** "Development of New Stereoisomeric Drugs", policy statement issued **1 May 1992** (Federal Register 57 FR 22249, 27 May 1992). [S] https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/development-new-stereoisomeric-drugs · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-01-15/html/97-944.htm
- **Share of approvals** (McVicker & O'Boyle, *J. Med. Chem.* 67 (2024) 2305, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.3c02239) [V Europe PMC full text]:
  - Racemic FDA small-molecule NME approvals: **23/211 = 11%** (2003–12) fell to **10/278 = 3.6%** (2013–22).
  - Single enantiomers: **57% → 59%** of new small-molecule approvals.
  - EMA has approved **no racemate since 2016**.
  - The often-repeated older claim ("~90% of chiral compounds marketed as racemates") is unsourced and outdated. Don't use it.
- **Chiral switches:** esomeprazole (Nexium, **2001**, from omeprazole), escitalopram (Lexapro, **2002**, from citalopram), armodafinil (2007), and others. Gellad et al., *Am J Manag Care* 20 (2014) e90 found "no evidence of superior efficacy over the older racemic precursors". [S] https://ajmc.com/view/assessing-the-chiral-switch-approval-and-use-of-single-enantiomer-drugs-2001-to-2011 → this is an honest caveat for the script.
- **L-DOPA (Knowles, Monsanto):** [Rh(R,R)-DiPAMP)(COD)]⁺BF₄⁻ hydrogenation gives the protected amino acid in **95% ee**. It was "the first commercialized catalytic asymmetric synthesis employing a chiral transition metal complex", **in operation since 1974**. [V] Nobel 2001 advanced information, https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-chemistryprize2001-2.pdf. Knowles's lecture says Monsanto switched to Kagan-inspired ligands, and DiPAMP gave about 95% ee. [V]
- **Takasago l-menthol (Japan):** a cationic **(S)-BINAP–Rh** complex isomerizes geranyldiethylamine to (R)-citronellal enamine. After hydrolysis this gives (R)-citronellal in **96–99% ee**, against about 80% ee for natural citronellal from rose oil. The asymmetric step runs on a **nine-ton scale**. Takasago makes about **1,500 t/yr** of terpenic substrates, most of which becomes **1,000 t/yr of (−)-menthol, "one-third of the world demand"**. Plant photo dated **February 1984**. The process came from Osaka Univ. (Otsuka, Tani), Nagoya (Noyori), IMS (Takaya), Shizuoka Univ. and Takasago. [V] Noyori Nobel lecture 2001, https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/noyori-lecture.pdf
  - Other figures: Akutagawa, *Top. Catal.* 4 (1997) 271 says "1,500 t/y scale of (−)-menthol" with TON > 400,000 [S]. A 2003 *Green Chem.* Noyori profile gives 2,000 t/yr total terpenes [S]. Wikipedia gives about 3,000 t/yr after a 2008 expansion [S].
  - **Say "about 1,000 tons a year (Noyori, 2001)".**

## UNVERIFIED / MYTHS
1. **Limonene orange/lemon = myth.** Analytical-grade enantiomers are not perceived as orange vs lemon (Kvittingen et al., *J. Chem. Educ.* 2021). Both citrus oils are mostly (R). Use carvone instead.
2. **"Pure R-thalidomide would have been safe" = myth.** Interconversion takes hours in vivo (Eriksson 1995). Also, the 1950s drug was racemic, and 1957–61 regulation had no stereochemical testing at all. The (S)/(R) teratogenicity split comes mostly from later work (Blaschke 1979 rodent data [M], which was contested), plus Mori 2018 cereblon binding (about 10× for S).
3. **"D = dextrorotatory, L = levorotatory" = wrong.** It is repeated even on Wikipedia *Homochirality*. D/L is configuration relative to glyceraldehyde.
4. **Kelvin "1904"**: the coinage is the **1893** lecture (printed 1894). The Baltimore Lectures of 1904 only reprint the footnote. "1873" (Rzepa page) is wrong.
5. **"Racemic acid from wine lees"**: racemic acid isn't in grape juice. Kestner obtained it at his Thann tartaric-acid works (1822) while processing tartar. Say "a by-product of making tartaric acid from wine tartar".
6. **Pasteur's temperature**: sources give 27.7 °C (Reusch), "below 28 °C" (OpenStax), or 28 °C as the dehydration point (Vantomme & Crassous). Use "about 27 °C / below 28 °C". The "cool Paris lab = luck" story was **not** found in any primary source. Say only that the salt and conditions were fortunate.
7. **Biot's quote** is a translation. The French original is "…que cela me fait battre le cœur". "Touches my very heart" is loose.
8. **Hajos–Parrish (−)-NLE** (1986) was later disputed (List/Houk 2003, Barbas found no NLE). Lead with the Sharpless epoxidation (+)-NLE example instead.
9. **Noyori DAIB "98% ee with pure DAIB"** is [M]. The 15% → 95% figure is confirmed.
10. **Thalidomide death fraction "~40%"** comes from Wikipedia (secondary). Totals vary: MHLW/いしずえ say "数千人～1万人" worldwide, Wikipedia says ">10,000, possibly 20,000". Say "1万人以上 (推定)" and cite the source on screen.
11. **Japan dates**: 17 May 1962 (shipment pause) and 13 Sept 1962 (recall start) come from a search snippet. The MHLW document itself only says "1962年9月 販売停止・回収開始". The 309 count is confirmed. The 171 male / 138 female split is from a snippet only.
12. **Soai 1995 table entry 5** (99% catalyst → 88%) was garbled in extraction. The four-round sequence 2 → 10 → 57 → 81 → 88 is clear.
13. **"Other group" reproducing absolute asymmetric synthesis** = Singleton & Vo, from memory, not checked.
14. **Nobel official press-release wording** was not fetched (403). Confirm it on nobelprize.org.
