## Cold open

Hey everyone.
Today: how a wrong turn, a stalled car and a nineteen-year-old outside a deli lit the fuse of the biggest war the world had ever seen.
But first, you need to know why Europe was sitting on that fuse in the first place.

By nineteen fourteen, the world had electric lights, telephones, cars and even airplanes, and the great powers of Europe were richer than ever.
Naturally, they decided the best use of all that progress was a contest to see who could build the biggest army, and the biggest navy.
In nineteen fourteen they finally got their war, and H. G. Wells, the War of the Worlds guy, hopefully called it "the war that will end war".
Today we just call it World War One.

## Then and now

This is the world map today, and here is the United States, minding its own business.
And this is the world in nineteen fourteen, when a handful of European empires ruled huge chunks of Africa and Asia.
That's imperialism: show up, plant a flag, and ship the good stuff home.
Britain ran about a fifth of all the land on Earth this way, and France and Russia were busy collecting too.

Germany, on the other hand, only became one country in eighteen seventy-one, and by nineteen fourteen it was making more steel than Britain.
But by the time Germany showed up to the colony party, the best snacks were gone, so it started demanding what it called "a place in the sun".

## Two gangs

So Europe split into two gangs.
Germany, Austria-Hungary, which was one empire, two countries and one very old emperor, and Italy formed the Triple Alliance, while Britain, France and Russia, getting nervous, teamed up as the Triple Entente.
Italy, still sulking because France had grabbed Tunisia, actually asked to join, and Germany said, "Sure, just don't flake on us," which, spoiler, Italy totally would.
Everyone promised, more or less, to back up their friends in a fight, which sounds sweet until you realize it turns one small fight into a giant one.
And the most dangerous spot was the Balkans, the crowded corner of southeast Europe, where Austria-Hungary had grabbed Bosnia in nineteen oh eight, and its neighbor Serbia, who wanted it too, was absolutely furious.
People called the Balkans the powder keg of Europe, and all it needed was a spark.

## The spark

That spark came from one wrong turn, and a driver nobody had bothered to brief.

June twenty-eighth, nineteen fourteen: Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to Austria-Hungary, rides through Sarajevo in an open car with his wife, Sophie.
Along the route wait six young Bosnians, armed with pistols, bombs and cyanide by a Serbian secret society called the Black Hand.

The first one throws a bomb, but it bounces off the folded roof and explodes under the car behind.
So he swallows his cyanide and jumps into the river,
but the poison is too old to work, and the river is five inches deep.
Plan failed, Archduke safe, World War One cancelled.

## But no

But no.
After complaining that he came here as a guest and got greeted with bombs, the Archduke decides to visit the wounded in hospital.
Nice of him, except nobody tells the driver that the route has changed.

So the cars turn onto the old street, the governor yells "Wrong way!", and while reversing, the engine stalls right outside a deli.
Standing right there is Gavrilo Princip, nineteen, one of the six, with his target parked five feet away.
And no, he wasn't buying a sandwich, that part is a myth.

## Two shots

He fires twice.
Franz Ferdinand and Sophie are dead within the hour.

## Aftermath

One month later, Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia, and within a week, half of Europe has joined in.
All because nobody told the driver? Not quite, but it sure didn't help.
Next time: one angry letter, one blank cheque, and seven days that dragged in half of Europe.
And Italy? Well, Italy has a decision to make.

    Small problem: nobody tells the driver about the new route.

    Wrong turn. He stops, tries to reverse… and stalls. Right outside a deli.
    Standing there: Gavrilo Princip. Nineteen. One of the six.

    ## Two shots

    Two shots.
    The Archduke and Sophie are killed.
